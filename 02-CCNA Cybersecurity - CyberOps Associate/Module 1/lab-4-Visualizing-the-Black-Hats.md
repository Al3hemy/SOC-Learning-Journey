# 🧪 Lab: Visualizing the Black Hats

## Objective

The goal of this lab was to analyze cyber attackers by creating hypothetical scenarios that reflect real-world attack patterns, motivations, and vulnerabilities.

---

## Introduction

In this lab, I explored how different types of attackers operate by putting myself in their position.

Instead of just learning definitions, I had to think through who the attacker is, what they want, how they attack, and how those attacks could be prevented. This made the whole concept of cyber threats feel more practical and realistic.

---

## 🕵️ Scenario 1: The Opportunistic Hacker

### Attacker

An amateur hacker (script kiddie) with basic technical knowledge.

### Affiliation

No formal organization, operates independently using publicly available tools.

### Motive

Curiosity and the desire to gain unauthorized access, possibly for recognition or minor financial gain.

### Method of Attack

Brute force attack on login systems using automated tools.

### Target & Vulnerability

- Target: Small business website  
- Vulnerability: Weak passwords and lack of account lockout mechanisms  

### Mitigation

- Enforce strong password policies  
- Implement account lockout after multiple failed attempts  
- Use multi-factor authentication (MFA)  

---

## 🕶️ Scenario 2: The Hacktivist Group

### Attacker

A hacktivist group targeting organizations based on ideology.

### Affiliation

Part of an online activist collective.

### Motive

To protest against the organization’s actions or beliefs and expose sensitive information.

### Method of Attack

Distributed Denial-of-Service (DDoS) attack to shut down services.

### Target & Vulnerability

- Target: Government or corporate website  
- Vulnerability: Lack of traffic filtering and weak infrastructure to handle large-scale requests  

### Mitigation

- Use DDoS protection services  
- Implement traffic monitoring and filtering  
- Strengthen network infrastructure  

---

## 🧠 Scenario 3: The Advanced Persistent Threat (APT)

### Attacker

A highly skilled and organized cybercriminal group.

### Affiliation

Possibly state-sponsored or part of a well-funded cybercrime organization.

### Motive

Long-term data exfiltration, espionage, or financial gain.

### Method of Attack

Phishing attack used to gain initial access, followed by malware installation.

### Target & Vulnerability

- Target: Large organization (e.g., financial institution)  
- Vulnerability: Human error (employees clicking malicious links)  

### Mitigation

- Employee cybersecurity awareness training  
- Email filtering and phishing detection systems  
- Endpoint security solutions  

---

## What I Learned

This lab helped me understand that:

- Different attackers have different motivations and skill levels  
- Not all attacks are purely technical, human behavior plays a big role  
- The same system can be targeted in multiple ways depending on the attacker
