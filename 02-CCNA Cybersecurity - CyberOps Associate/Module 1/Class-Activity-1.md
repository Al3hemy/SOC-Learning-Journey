# 🧠 Activity: Hacker Demonstration Analysis

## Objective

The goal of this activity was to observe how real-world attacks happen and to understand the thought process behind exploiting vulnerabilities.

---

## What I Observed

From the video, one thing that really stood out to me is how devices we use every day are actually part of a network.

For example, even a television is a node on a network. That means it can be targeted just like a computer. If someone has the right level of skill, they can send malicious code through the network and exploit the TV.

This could allow them to:
- Access features without authorization (like free content)  
- Monitor activity on the device  
- Potentially capture sensitive information being displayed  

This completely changed how I see everyday devices. They’re not just appliances, they’re potential entry points.

---

## 📡 Focus: RFID Credit Card Vulnerability

What struck me the most in this activity was the concept of RFID-enabled credit cards.

We generally see them as secure because of the technology they use, but I learned that this same feature can become a weakness if not properly protected.

---

## a. Vulnerability Being Exploited

The vulnerability here is **unauthorized RFID scanning**.

Since RFID cards are designed to communicate wirelessly with scanners, they can also be read by unauthorized devices if proper safeguards are not in place.

---

## b. What Can Be Gained

If this vulnerability is exploited, a hacker can gain access to:

- Financial information stored on the card  
- Sensitive transaction-related data  

---

## c. How the Attack Works

The attack relies on the fact that RFID chips respond to scanners.

An attacker uses a rogue scanner to read the card without the owner’s knowledge. Instead of going through secure payment systems, they bypass those controls and directly extract the data.

---

## d. What Interested Me Most

What I found most
 interesting is the contradiction.

We carry these cards because they are considered secure and convenient, but at the same time, that convenience makes them vulnerable. It made me realize that not all security features are completely safe.

---

## e. Possible Mitigation

One practical way to reduce this risk is by using **RFID-blocking wallets**.

These help prevent unauthorized scanners from accessing the card unless it is intentionally used.
## Key Takeaway

Security is not just about what a system is designed to do, but also about how it can be misused.
