# 🛡️ Module 1: The Danger

## Introduction

This module was my first real exposure to how serious cybersecurity is. Before this, I knew attacks happened, but I didn’t fully understand *why* they happen or how often systems are exposed.

Now I see networks differently. Anything connected is a potential target, and security is something that has to be intentional, not assumed.

---

## 🎯 Objective

The goal of this module was to understand why networks and data are attacked.

From what I learned, attacks are usually not random. Attackers are often trying to:

* Gain access to sensitive data
* Take control of systems
* Exploit weaknesses for financial or strategic reasons

This made me realize that every system has value to someone, even if it doesn’t seem obvious at first.

---

## 📚 Core Concepts Covered

To understand attacks, I first had to understand how systems work.

* **Virtual Machines**
  I learned how to set up virtual machines, which act like a safe testing environment. This is important because it allows me to experiment without risking a real system.

* **Operating Systems (Linux)**
  I started exploring how operating systems function, especially Linux. It made me realize that attackers often take advantage of system-level weaknesses.

* **Network Protocols & Infrastructure**
  These define how devices communicate. Understanding them helped me see that communication itself can be exploited if not secured properly.

* **Network Attacks**
  I was introduced to different types of attacks and how they target weaknesses in systems.

* **Cryptography**
  This showed me how important encryption is. Without it, data would be easily exposed during transmission.

---

## 🎥 Learning from Real Attacks

Watching a hacker demonstration was one of the most interesting parts of this module.

Instead of just watching, I started analyzing the attack by asking:

* What vulnerability is being exploited?
* What does the attacker gain?
* How was the attack carried out?
* How could it have been prevented?

This helped me understand that hacking follows a process, not just random actions.

---

## ⚔️ Key Insight

One simple idea that stuck with me:

* Public or unsecured Wi-Fi networks are high-risk environments

It sounds basic, but it really changed how I think about everyday internet usage.

---

## 🕵️ Threat Actors

Not all attackers are the same, and understanding this changes how you think about threats.

* **Script Kiddies (Amateurs)**
  They use existing tools without deep knowledge but can still cause damage.

* **Hacktivists**
  These attackers are driven by political or social reasons rather than money.

This made me realize that motivation plays a big role in how attacks happen.

---

## 🌐 Internet of Things (IoT)

This part made me look at everyday devices differently.

A lot of smart devices are vulnerable, especially if security wasn’t considered when they were built. Some key concerns include:

* Who developed the firmware?
* Was security considered during development?
* Can the device be updated or patched?

If the answer to these is weak, the device becomes an easy target.

---

## 🔍 Case Study: Dyn Attack

The Dyn attack showed how powerful large-scale attacks can be.

It involved multiple compromised devices working together to overwhelm systems. What stood out to me is that many of these devices were just regular internet-connected gadgets.

It made me realize that small vulnerabilities can combine into something much bigger.

---

## ⚠️ Threat Impact

This module also covered the types of data attackers usually target:

* **PII (Personally Identifiable Information):** Names, emails, etc.
* **PHI (Protected Health Information):** Medical records
* **PSI (Personal Security Information):** Sensitive personal data

This made cybersecurity feel more real because these are things that directly affect people.

---

## 💡 What I Took Away

If I had to sum up this module simply:

* Systems are only as strong as their weakest point
* Attackers look for opportunities, not perfection
* Security is more about mindset than tools

I’m starting to naturally think in terms of *“where could this break?”* instead of just *“this works fine.”*
